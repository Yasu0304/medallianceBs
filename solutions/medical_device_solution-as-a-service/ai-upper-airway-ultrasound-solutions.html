AI-Powered Upper Airway Ultrasound Assessment & Clinical Integration Solutions

Combining AI-powered imaging analysis with non-invasive ultrasound technology to enable faster and more informed obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) risk assessment.

Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA) is a common yet frequently underrecognized sleep-related breathing disorder. While conventional sleep studies provide essential physiological data, upper airway ultrasound offers additional insights into airway anatomy and surrounding soft tissue structures, supporting a more comprehensive clinical evaluation.

MedAlliance provides AI-powered upper airway ultrasound assessment and clinical integration solutions that combine standardized ultrasound scanning, AI-assisted image analysis, and quantitative measurements. This approach enables healthcare professionals to obtain valuable upper airway anatomical information in approximately 10 minutes, supporting OSA risk assessment and subsequent clinical decision-making.

Through medical device deployment and clinical workflow integration, MedAlliance helps healthcare providers expand opportunities in sleep health screening, preventive care, and specialized clinical services.

Key Solution Advantages

10-Minute Rapid Assessment:
Obtain upper airway images while patients are awake, improving examination convenience and workflow efficiency.

AI-Powered Image Analysis: Provide quantitative information on airway dimensions and surrounding soft tissue characteristics.

Non-Invasive Ultrasound Technology: Enable examinations without ionizing radiation or anesthesia.

Standardized Scan Positioning: Support consistent image acquisition through assisted positioning technology.

Clinical Decision Support: Provide anatomical information associated with OSA risk to assist further examinations and follow-up planning.

Healthcare Workflow Integration: Support the planning of examination workflows and device deployment based on the needs of different healthcare facilities.

Clinical Applications

Sleep Medicine & ENT Clinics

Upper airway imaging and quantitative tissue analysis help physicians evaluate patient-specific anatomical characteristics, providing additional information for OSA risk assessment, specialized clinical evaluation, and further diagnostic planning.

Health Screening & Preventive Care Centers

Integrating non-invasive upper airway ultrasound assessment into health screening programs can help identify individuals with potential OSA risk and support subsequent sleep health management and specialist referrals.

Personalized Sleep-Disordered Breathing Management

Upper airway anatomical and soft tissue information supports individualized clinical assessment and provides quantitative data for treatment-related evaluations, follow-up observations, and clinical research.

From Imaging to Clinical Assessment: An Integrated Workflow

Standardized Ultrasound Scanning → AI Image Analysis → Quantitative Assessment → Clinical Interpretation & Follow-Up Management

By combining standardized image acquisition with AI-assisted quantitative analysis, healthcare professionals can obtain more consistent upper airway anatomical information. These findings can be evaluated alongside patient symptoms, medical history, and other clinical examination results to support appropriate follow-up assessments and care planning.

Recommended Clinical Workflow

Step 1
Assessment of Clinical Needs

Review patient symptoms, medical history, and existing risk assessment tools to determine the need for upper airway imaging.

Step 2
Standardized Ultrasound Scanning

Perform upper airway ultrasound imaging while the patient is awake, using laser-assisted positioning to support standardized image acquisition.

Step 3
AI Image Analysis & Reporting

Analyze upper airway ultrasound images to provide information on tissue morphology, airway dimensions, and related quantitative measurements, supporting clinical risk assessment.

Step 4
Clinical Interpretation & Follow-Up Management

Healthcare professionals interpret the imaging findings alongside patient symptoms and other examination results to determine appropriate diagnostic evaluations, specialist referrals, or follow-up plans.
